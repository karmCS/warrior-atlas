Hironori Otsuka came to karate already a master of something else. He started training in Shindo Yoshin-ryu jujutsu at thirteen under Tatsusaburo Nakayama, and by June 1921 he held menkyo kaiden, full mastery and a license to teach, in that jujutsu tradition. He didn't meet Gichin Funakoshi until the following year, when Funakoshi arrived in Tokyo teaching Okinawan karate to a mainland audience that barely knew the art existed. Otsuka trained under him and was among the first students Funakoshi promoted to black belt, in 1924, but he also studied with Kenwa Mabuni and Choki Motobu, picking up approaches to free sparring that Funakoshi's kata-centered curriculum didn't cover.

What Otsuka built from these threads was not Funakoshi's karate with jujutsu bolted on. He kept the striking but reworked the footwork around his jujutsu's evasive body shifting, tai sabaki, and folded in joint locks and throws, so a defender slips an attack's line rather than meeting it head-on. He opened his own school in 1934, and the name Wado-ryu, "way of harmony," stuck soon after. The Dai Nippon Butoku Kai registered his style in 1938, and when it required all schools to formally register their names the following year, other major lines, including Funakoshi's own Shotokan, went through the same process. Whether Wado-ryu was the first karate style recognized independently of its founder's organization, as some accounts claim, is harder to confirm than that framing suggests.
