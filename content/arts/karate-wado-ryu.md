Wado-ryu does not meet force with force. Where most karate stops an incoming strike with a hard block, Wado-ryu moves the whole body off the line the strike is travelling on, a shift called tai sabaki that evades and loads the counter in the same motion. That idea belongs to jujutsu rather than Okinawa, and it brings the rest of jujutsu with it; joint locks and throws sit in the syllabus beside the striking, and a practitioner is expected to end up behind an attacker rather than in front of one. The name translates to the way of harmony, which for once is a description.

Hironori Otsuka came to karate already a master of something else. He started Shindo Yoshin-ryu jujutsu at thirteen and by June 1921 held menkyo kaiden, full mastery and a licence to teach. He did not meet Gichin Funakoshi until the following year, and was among the first students Funakoshi promoted to black belt in 1924, though he also trained with Kenwa Mabuni and Choki Motobu, who had things to say about free sparring that a kata-centred syllabus did not cover. His own school opened in 1934.
