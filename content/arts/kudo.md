Takashi Azuma was born May 22, 1949, in Kesennuma, Miyagi Prefecture. He joined his high school's judo club at 16, then, after military service, joined Kyokushinkaikan, Mas Oyama's karate organization, in 1971 while studying at Waseda University. He founded and captained Waseda's Kyokushin club in 1972, fighting in Kyokushin's All Japan tournaments through the decade: runner-up in 1974, third in 1976, and champion of the 9th All Japan Karate Championship in 1977, plus sixth at the first World Tournament in 1975 and fourth at the second in 1979. Fighters called him "the human locomotive" for his low kicks.

Azuma retired from competition in 1979 and took over Kyokushin's Miyagi branch. Facing larger opponents abroad convinced him Kyokushin's rules, banning face punches and throws, left smaller fighters no way to close the gap; he wanted face strikes under headgear, groin strikes, and judo throws added. His autobiography also cites personal grievances, including a bout that left his nose bent. On February 17, 1981, he founded Karate-do Daido Juku in Sendai, Miyagi's capital, blending Kyokushin striking with judo grappling; boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, and jujitsu technique entered through the 1980s and 1990s. Azuma renamed the system Kudo in 2001 and ran its first world championship that year; the Kudo International Federation now governs it. He held ninth dan in Kudo, eighth dan in Jon Bluming's Kyokushin Budokai, and third dan in judo. He died of stomach cancer on April 3, 2021; Kenichi Osada succeeded him as Daido Juku president.
