Masutatsu Oyama, born Choi Yeong-eui in Japanese-occupied Korea in 1923, moved to Japan as a teenager and trained under Gichin Funakoshi at Takushoku University, studying Shotokan for about two years. He also spent several years under So Nei Chu, a fellow Korean and senior Goju-ryu student of founder Chojun Miyagi, and later trained with Gogen Yamaguchi of the Goju-kai. That combination of Shotokan's linear striking and Goju-ryu's closer-range power shaped the style Oyama began teaching from a dojo near Rikkyo University in 1953. He called it Kyokushinkai by 1957, and in April 1964 formally founded the International Karate Organization Kyokushin kaikan, with a headquarters dojo completed that June in Ikebukuro, Tokyo.

Kyokushin's signature is "knockdown karate": full-contact matches fought bare-fisted, with kicks and knees allowed to the head and full-power strikes to the body and legs, while punches to the head are barred. Oyama also built his name on legend. He claimed to have fought and killed dozens of bulls bare-handed and to have beaten 100 opponents in a single day's kumite; former students have called both claims staged or mathematically impossible, and most historians treat them as promotional myth rather than fact. The knockdown format he pioneered later shaped Kazuyoshi Ishii's Seidokaikan, which fed into the K-1 kickboxing circuit Ishii founded in 1993, and Azuma Takashi's Kudo, which started as a Kyokushin-judo hybrid before Azuma broke away in the early 1980s.
