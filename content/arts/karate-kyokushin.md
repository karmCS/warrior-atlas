Kyokushin fights bare-knuckle and trains the same way. No gloves, no shin pads, no headgear, so the hands and shins have to be conditioned into the weapons the rules already assume they are. Matches are full contact with kicks and knees allowed to the head and full power to the body and legs, but no punches to the face at all, because bare fists on an unprotected head would end bouts in seconds. That single exclusion shapes everything else. With no hand threat to the head, fighters stand close and trade, nobody has much reason to move backwards, and a bout is decided by body shots and low kicks landing until somebody's legs stop working, which is why the format is called knockdown karate.

Masutatsu Oyama was born Choi Yeong-eui in occupied Korea in 1923, studied Shotokan under Gichin Funakoshi, then spent several years with So Nei Chu, a senior Goju-ryu student of Chōjun Miyagi's. Shotokan's straight lines plus Goju-ryu's close-range power is roughly what he began teaching in 1953, founding the organization in April 1964. The format fed Seidokaikan, K-1 in 1993, and Kudo, and Kyokushin karateka like ONE FC's Yuki Yoza and Hiroki Akimoto are still among the most dangerous kickboxers competing.
