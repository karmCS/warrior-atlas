Wing Chun is a close-quarters Southern Chinese martial art built around centerline theory, simultaneous attack and defense, and trapping hand techniques. Its origin is unresolved: Wikipedia's own entry notes that no definitive founder has been identified, and at least eight lineages tell different versions of how the style began. The most repeated account holds that a Shaolin nun, Ng Mui, taught the style to a young woman named Yim Wing-chun in the late Ming or early Qing period, but Wikipedia calls Yim Wing-chun the mythical originator of the art, and the story is generally treated as legend rather than documented history.

The earliest well-documented practitioner is Leung Jan (1821-1901), a Dit Da herbalist who ran a clinic in Foshan and began teaching Wing Chun privately around 1870, after building a reputation through challenge matches. His student Chan Wah-shun later taught a young Ip Man, who by most accounts started training around age 9 or 13 (sources disagree). Ip Man was born in Foshan in 1893 and moved his family to Hong Kong in 1949 or 1950, depending on the source, where he taught openly for the first time. His students included Bruce Lee, who built Jeet Kune Do on Wing Chun's trapping and centerline concepts. Wing Chun techniques were later added to the German Jujutsu curriculum in 2000.
