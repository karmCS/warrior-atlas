Chōjun Miyagi (1888-1953) founded Goju-ryu in Naha, Okinawa, building on Naha-te, the style he learned under Kanryō Higaonna starting at age fourteen. Higaonna had trained in Fuzhou, China, during the 1870s, and Miyagi made his own trips to Fujian province in 1915 and 1917 to study local martial arts. He trained under Higaonna for fifteen years, until Higaonna's death in 1916, then spent the next decade shaping what he had learned into a distinct system.

The name came later, and almost by accident. In 1929, Miyagi's student Jin'an Shinzato demonstrated the art at a martial event and had no answer when asked its name, since Naha-te had never been a formal school title. Miyagi settled on "Goju-ryu," hard-soft style, taking the words from a line in the Bubishi's "Eight Poems of the Fist," roughly "the way of inhaling and exhaling is hardness and softness" (some sources put the formal naming in 1930 instead). The style pairs linear, closed-fist strikes with circular, open-hand redirection and close-range grappling, built around Sanchin kata and its controlled breathing method.

Miyagi's influence reached past his own school. Mas Oyama, who later founded Kyokushin, trained for several years under So Nei Chu, a senior Goju-ryu student of Miyagi's, and also studied under Gōgen Yamaguchi, who led Miyagi's style on the Japanese mainland. Kenwa Mabuni, founder of Shito-ryu, studied under Higaonna too, introduced to him by Miyagi, making Goju-ryu and Shito-ryu siblings through a shared teacher rather than direct descent.
