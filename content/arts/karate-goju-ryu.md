Goju-ryu means hard-soft, and both halves are literal. The hard is straight closed-fist striking, and the soft is circular open-hand work that takes an incoming arm off its line instead of stopping it. It is fought close, inside the other person's reach, where the circles are small and there is nowhere to retreat to, so the answer to pressure is to redirect it rather than back away. All of it is organized around Sanchin, a short kata performed slowly under muscular tension with audible controlled breathing, which is the style's conditioning method and its signature at once.

Chōjun Miyagi built it out of Naha-te, which he began learning at fourteen under Kanryō Higaonna, and he made his own training trips to Fujian in 1915 and 1917. The name arrived almost by accident. In 1929 his student Jin'an Shinzato was asked at a demonstration what the art was called and had no answer, because Naha-te had never been a school name, so Miyagi took Goju from a line in the Bubishi about inhaling and exhaling. Mas Oyama trained under his senior student So Nei Chu before founding Kyokushin.
