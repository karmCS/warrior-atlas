Kickboxing has two distinct lineages, albeit both being similar in style.

In Japan, the term "kickboxing" was first coined by Japanese boxing promoter Osamu Noguchi. The discipline itself, however, originated from Karate master Tatsuo Yamada. Inspired by the growth of Muay Thai and its similarities to Karate, Yamada looked to challenge his karateka students with Thailand's best fighters, thus prompting Noguchi to organize their first cross-discipline, full-contact sparring tournament between Japanese and Thai fighters in 1963. As the sport grew, Japanese fighters began trading techniques with the Thais and vice-versa. The rules began to mold as well, removing the Thai clinch from competition to favor a more forward-marching and faster paced fight than traditional Muay Thai's heavy footed competitions.

Today, kickboxing has grown into its own discipline, with schools opening up across the world teaching techniques derived from Muay Thai and Kickboxing, as well as borrowing from Taekwondo and Boxing. It is quite often you find fighters from different Karate disciplines, such as ONE FC's Yuki Yoza or Hiroki Akimoto (both trained in Kyokushin Karate) as well as Muay Thai fighters like Jonathan Haggerty and Tawanchai challenge themselves in kickboxing competitions.
