ITF Taekwondo is the older and more traditional of the sport's two branches, organized around its patterns. There are twenty-four of them, tul, one for each hour of the day, and they hold the syllabus the way kata hold karate's. The technical argument that separates this branch from the Olympic one is a motion called sine wave, where the body rises and then drops through each technique, on the theory that dropping one's own mass into a strike generates power differently from the hip snap Seoul teaches. Sparring is light-contact and fought at long range with the hands kept low, because at that distance the legs get there first.

Taekwondo got its name in 1955, when the leaders of Korea's postwar kwans agreed to unify, and General Choi Hong Hi pushed hardest for it. The Korea Taekwondo Association formalized the merger in 1959, and Choi walked out in 1966 to found the International Taekwon-Do Federation, adding sine wave around 1980. The politics never left the art. After Seoul blocked him from teaching in North Korea he moved his headquarters to Toronto in 1972 and Vienna in 1985, and he died in Pyongyang in 2002.
