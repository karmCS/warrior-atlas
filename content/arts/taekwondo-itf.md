Taekwondo took its name and shape between 1955 and 1959, as leaders of Korea's postwar kwans, schools rooted largely in Japanese-era karate, agreed to unify under one name and curriculum. Choi Hong Hi, an army general who rose from lieutenant to major general within a decade, pushed hardest for the merger; the name landed on April 11, 1955. How much of this was indigenous versus adapted karate stays disputed: the government's traditionalist account and newer revisionist scholarship disagree, a debate predating the split here. The Korea Taekwondo Association formalized the merger in 1959, but Choi broke away in 1966 to found the International Taekwon-Do Federation in Seoul, keeping control of a curriculum built around 24 named patterns, or tul.

Around 1980 Choi added sine wave, a knee-driven down-up-down motion meant to generate power differently than the snap-based style of the Seoul-based mainstream, a technical break still argued over inside ITF schools. In 1972, after Seoul blocked the ITF from teaching in North Korea, Choi went into exile and moved headquarters to Toronto; the ITF relocated again to Vienna in 1985, neutral ground that let Choi build ties on both sides of the Cold War, North Korea included. Choi died in Pyongyang in 2002, and rival ITF factions have competed since. Wikidata now lists Austria as the ITF's country, reflecting Vienna's registration rather than where the art or the organization began. Seoul's mainstream body, [[taekwondo-wtf]], is a sibling of the same 1955-59 unification, not a descendant of the ITF.
