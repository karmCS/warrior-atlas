Wrestling is the art of controlling a person without hurting them, and it is the most borrowed system in all of combat sport. No strikes, no chokes, no joint locks; the entire contest is position. Take the opponent down, turn them, and in freestyle and folkstyle end it by pinning both shoulders to the mat. Grips are taken on the body rather than on clothing, so nothing slows an exchange down. Every system that ends up on the ground has to answer the question of who gets to be on top, and wrestling is the answer all of them use.

It has no founder and no homeland, appearing in cave art in France fifteen thousand years ago and in the ancient Olympics by 708 BCE. Since no country can claim it, this entry follows documented dominance instead. Georgia's chidaoba carries roughly 200 named holds and is still its most successful Olympic event, and the Soviet system took 116 Olympic medals, a legacy sharpest in Dagestan, a republic of about 3.1 million people with more than twenty Olympic golds since 1992, which is why the marker sits on Makhachkala.
