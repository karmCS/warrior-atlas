Shito-ryu is the karate style that refused to choose. Shuri-te is direct and driving, worked at arm's length, while Naha-te is tighter, more circular, and fought close, with breathing and tension work Shuri-te has no use for. Most Okinawan karateka of that generation picked one and stayed inside it. Kenwa Mabuni kept both, which is why a Shito-ryu student learns long-range lines and short-range circles side by side, and why the style carries an unusually long kata list, somewhere between fifty and ninety depending on the branch.

Mabuni started under Ankō Itosu in Shuri at about thirteen, and his friend Chōjun Miyagi later introduced him to Kanryō Higaonna, so he took up Naha-te seriously as well. He moved to Osaka in 1929 to teach on the mainland and first called his approach Hanko-ryu, half-hard style, before settling on Shitō-ryū around 1931, assembled from the readings of the first characters in his two teachers' names: Shi from Itosu, Tō from Higaonna. It is one of the four styles recognized by the World Karate Federation.
