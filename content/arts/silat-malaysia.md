Malaysian silat has no agreed founder or founding date. UNESCO's 2019 listing of Malaysia's "Silat" traces it to the Langkasuka kingdom on the northern Malay Peninsula, with animal-derived movements said to date back roughly 2,000 years. Wikidata has no coordinates for either Langkasuka or the Silat Melayu entry, so the location marked here is Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia's capital, not a confirmed birthplace. The art took clearer shape under the 15th-century Melaka Sultanate, when Sufi teachers folded Islamic philosophy into training and Hang Tuah became its best-known folk hero; the Hikayat Hang Tuah's kris duel between Hang Tuah and his sworn brother Hang Jebat is cited as silat's most famous literary fight.

Named regional styles solidified later. Silat Cekak formed in the Kedah royal court, reportedly to counter Siamese fighters' tomoi (muay Thai), and Silat Seni Gayong was registered in 1963 by Meor Abdul Rahman. The national federation PESAKA formed in 1983 to unite these bodies, and a standardized "Seni Silat Malaysia" school curriculum followed in 2002.

Whether silat began in Malaysia or Indonesia remains unsettled, and Wikidata's own "Silat Melayu" entry lists Indonesia, not Malaysia, as its country of origin, despite describing Malay Peninsula styles. UNESCO avoided ruling on the question by inscribing Indonesia's pencak silat and Malaysia's silat as separate listings on the same day in December 2019. This entry treats Indonesian pencak silat, [[silat-indonesia]], as a parallel sibling tradition, not a technical ancestor: the two share deep, contested roots rather than one demonstrably descending from the other.
