Kickboxing has two distinct lineages, albeit both being similar in style.

The origin of American Kickboxing is far different from Japanese Kickboxing. Born in the 1970s, point-karate fighters grew tired of stopping the fight after every scored point. They felt that the essence of a true fight was beyond point-based rules and wanted to challenge themselves as they were inspired by the rise of boxing. The Professional Karate Association (PKA) instituted strict "above-the-waist" striking rules and banned low kicks (a technique commonly seen in Asian kickboxing). This, however, led to a high-volume, dynamic kicking spectacle. The bladed Karate stance combined with the coaching from American boxers led to historical striking matches that many modern day fighters study today.
