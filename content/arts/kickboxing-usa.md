On January 17, 1970, karate champion Joe Lewis stepped into a ring in Long Beach, California, wearing boxing gloves instead of fighting under point-karate rules. Lewis had trained in Shōbayashi and Matsubayashi Shōrin-ryū karate on Okinawa and trained privately under Bruce Lee in 1967 and 1968. At the 1st Pro Team Karate Championships, he knocked out Kenpo stylist Greg "Om" Baines in the second round. The ring announcer, short on a better word for it, called the fighters "kickboxers," and the name stuck.

Four years later, Don and Judy Quine and promoter Mike Anderson founded the Professional Karate Association in Los Angeles, after an earlier body had folded in 1972. The PKA's first World Championships ran at the Los Angeles Sports Arena that September, with Lewis winning the heavyweight title; ABC broadcast it that December on Wide World of Entertainment. The PKA later ran for years on ESPN. Its rules kept boxing gloves and timed rounds but required a minimum number of kicks above the waist each round. Howard Hanson and Arnold Urquidez set up a rival body, the World Kickboxing Association, in 1976.

None of this traces back to Japan, where a full-contact mix of karate and Muay Thai had already been running since the mid-1960s under different promoters. The two branches share a root in karate but were built independently, about a decade apart and an ocean apart. See [[kickboxing-japan]] for the other half of the split.
