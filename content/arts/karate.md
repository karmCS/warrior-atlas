Karate is built around a single decisive strike, which is what separates it from the volume striking of boxing or Muay Thai. Power comes from the hips and the floor rather than the arm, travels in a straight line, and is pulled back as fast as it was sent, so the same limb is a threat again immediately. Even the blocks are strikes, aimed at the attacking arm rather than at absorbing the blow. Most of the material lives in kata, solo sequences run against imagined opponents, which is how an art with almost nothing written down survived several centuries intact.

It grew on Okinawa, then the independent Ryukyu Kingdom, out of a native practice called te crossed with Chinese methods carried back from Fujian, taking different shapes in Shuri, Naha, and Tomari. Anko Itosu put it into Shuri's school PE classes in 1901, and his student Gichin Funakoshi carried it to mainland Japan in the 1920s, borrowing judo's uniform and coloured belts along the way. In 1933 the character for kara was swapped from the one meaning China to a homophone meaning empty. Every major style below descends from it, and so do both branches of Taekwondo and Japanese kickboxing.
